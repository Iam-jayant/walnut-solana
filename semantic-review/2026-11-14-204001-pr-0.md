# Confidential lending architecture has unresolved accounting, liquidation, and recovery hazards

The specifications define a coherent two-phase confidential lending model, but several cross-phase invariants are not strong enough for mainnet custody. The most serious flaws let a queued debt operation settle against a different Borrow Index than the circuit used, let just-in-time suppliers capture already-accrued interest, and leave `view_position` without an owner-authorization requirement. Liquidation can be forced out of the sealed auction, reserve withdrawal can break epoch accounting, and the operational model has no demonstrated capacity or Arcium migration path. **Watch for: critical, confirmed specification gaps in async index consistency, epoch value attribution, and viewing-key authorization; high, confirmed gaps in auction admission, liquidation math, oracle defenses, callback failure handling, governance, privacy, and liveness.**

**Verdict**: NEEDS_CHANGES

## High-level view

The per-account Lock protects encrypted ciphertext and nonce pairs, but it does not protect the plaintext Market inputs consumed by a queued circuit. Borrow, repay, health, and settlement computations can therefore finalize after the Borrow Index or risk configuration has changed, breaking the assumption that the callback applies one coherent state transition.

Epoch accounting has two independent value-attribution problems. A supplier can mint shares immediately before a reveal and receive interest earned before the supply, while reserve withdrawal changes gross assets without being represented in the stated interest baseline. The operation-count gate also permits indefinite stale utilization and exact-delta privacy attacks.

The eight-slot first-come auction is cheap to exhaust, and the allowed fallback bonus can make a USDC-collateralized position less healthy after liquidation. A single transient Pyth spot update can trigger or settle that path because there is no deviation circuit breaker or secondary price policy.

The privacy boundary is incomplete because `view_position` can re-seal a position to a supplied key without an explicit owner check. Epoch totals also reveal exact aggregates after only five operations, a threshold an observer can pad with known dust operations.

The Arcium integration remains conditional where mainnet behavior must be fixed. Verification-failure cleanup conflicts with the documented error path, all pool activity is serialized behind one MPC round trip, and no cluster migration or key-recovery runbook protects users from a prolonged MXE outage.

Governance and operations depend on a Squads multisig and one scanning keeper without enforceable delays, capacity targets, liquidation incentives, or degraded-mode behavior.

5. **High · Auction slot exhaustion (confirmed)** — Zero and below-reserve bids can consume the fixed eight slots and force public fallback. Reject zero-capacity use, limit or replace bids per bidder, and add Sybil-resistant admission.

8. **High · Active epoch differencing leak (confirmed)** — An observer can pad a five-operation epoch with known dust actions and isolate a target amount. Require distinct economic cohorts and delayed/coarsened disclosure, or explicitly withdraw the amount-confidentiality guarantee.

## Queue-time debt math can settle against a different Market version

**Critical, confirmed.** Requirements 2.2–2.4 lock only an “encrypted-state account” and validate callback offsets (`requirements.md:170-175`), while the design explicitly says “Prices, indexes, and parameters are plaintext” (`design.md:155-159`). The borrow and repay circuits consume an index passed at queue time (`design.md:171-173`), but `accrue_interest` remains a separate permissionless instruction and “runs … before any use of debt” (`requirements.md:294-297`; `design.md:229-238`). No requirement locks or versions the per-asset Market fields until callback.

A repay queued at index `I0` burns `floor(payment / I0)` shares. If interest accrues to `I1` before callback, those shares remove `floor(payment / I0) × I1` of current debt while only `payment` enters Pool Cash. Parameter changes create the same split-brain behavior for health decisions, and stale queue-time prices can remain actionable after the oracle policy changes. This violates conservation even though the Position and Pool ciphertexts stayed locked.

The architecture must introduce a per-asset state version covering Borrow Index, Supply Index, oracle observations, and all risk parameters. A callback must either prove the version is unchanged or produce no state change and release through an authenticated stale-result path. Debt-changing computations also need an index lease that prevents accrual until callback, or a callback-safe rebase design whose conservation equations are formally specified and property-tested.

## Epoch timing assigns past interest to new suppliers and lets borrowers suppress future rates

**Critical, confirmed.** Supply shares mint at the current Supply Index (`requirements.md:230-232`), but that index changes only at an Epoch and the minimum supply duration defaults to zero (`requirements.md:237`). Epochs use the end-state shares and assets (`design.md:240-254`), and utilization for the next rate comes from the latest revealed totals (`requirements.md:294-305`).

A large supplier can enter immediately before an eligible reveal at the stale index. The reveal then distributes interest accumulated before that deposit across both old and newly minted shares, transferring part of incumbent suppliers’ accrued yield to the new supplier. The same supplier can withdraw immediately after the reveal, leaving a lower revealed utilization and therefore a lower borrow rate for the next epoch. A borrower can coordinate both positions and buy an epoch of underpriced debt with only short-lived liquidity.

Share minting and burning must checkpoint accrued supplier value before changing the denominator, or use continuously accrued supplier indexes with time-weighted accounting. Utilization must be time-weighted or derived from a manipulation-resistant observation window, not a single end-of-epoch balance. A nonzero holding period alone reduces but does not correctly attribute previously earned interest.

## `view_position` lacks the authorization rule that protects every balance

**Critical, confirmed.** Requirement 3.7 says the program will “re-seal[] the groups to a supplied Viewing Key” for rotation and recovery, then only states that the holder of that new secret can decrypt (`requirements.md:191-193`). The design exposes `view_position` as a computation and repeats that it re-seals all Position groups to a supplied key (`design.md:134`, `design.md:163-174`). In contrast, disclosure explicitly rejects every signer other than the Position owner (`requirements.md:351-355`). There is no equivalent authorization criterion for `view_position`.

An implementation faithful to this interface can let an arbitrary signer supply their own x25519 key and ask the MXE to re-encrypt another user’s balances, collateral, debt, tier, and repayment count. That is a complete confidentiality failure even if no funds move.

Require the Position owner’s wallet signature, bind the target Position PDA and new key into the signed instruction, and atomically update the registered viewing public key only after a successful rotation callback. Recovery must define a separate authenticated policy; possession of an arbitrary replacement key is not recovery authorization.

## Reserve withdrawal is absent from the epoch interest baseline

**High, confirmed.** The stated formula is `interest = assets_end - assets_start - net_supply_flow + bad_debt_written_off`, and `net_supply_flow` tracks only supplies minus withdrawals (`design.md:240-254`). Reserve withdrawal then reduces both cash and reserves and is asserted to preserve supplier claims (`design.md:271-275`; `requirements.md:308`). No term adjusts `assets_start` or records protocol outflow.

If the previous gross assets were 100 and the admin withdraws 5 of reserves with no other activity, the next epoch sees gross assets of 95 and computes negative interest of 5. With unsigned arithmetic this aborts and can make every future epoch fail; with signed handling it incorrectly changes reserves again. The final `assets - reserves` identity does not repair the incorrect period-interest calculation used for the reserve-factor split.

Track protocol inflows and outflows in the epoch accumulator, or snapshot supplier equity rather than gross assets. Reserve withdrawal must update the epoch baseline atomically, and properties must cover withdrawal immediately before and after a reveal, including zero and negative economic-interest periods.

## Eight first-come bid slots can be filled with zero or deliberately invalid bids

**High, confirmed.** The auction “accept[s] up to 8 encrypted Bids” and turns an underfunded bid into zero (`requirements.md:324-331`). The design assigns every slot `b[i]`, explicitly including `0 if the bidder’s balance did not cover it` (`design.md:256-263`). Neither document limits one slot per bidder, replaces a bidder’s prior bid, requires a public bond, nor avoids incrementing the bid count for zero or below-reserve values.

An attacker can occupy all slots before honest liquidators arrive, using underfunded bids or cheap funded bids known to be far below plausible reserve. Settlement then reports no valid bid, reveals the borrower’s debt and collateral, and enters the slower fallback. The attacker can systematically defeat the protocol’s sealed-price-discovery and privacy claims during volatile markets.

Do not consume scarce capacity for zero escrow. Require one replaceable bid per bidder plus a meaningful public anti-spam bond or nonrefundable submission fee, and add a permissionless capacity mechanism such as a bounded top-bid heap, commit/reveal admission, or more slots sized from measured demand. Tests must model Sybil slot exhaustion, not only winner selection over a preselected array.

## Allowed liquidation parameters can make partial liquidation worsen health

**High, confirmed.** USDC’s default liquidation threshold is 90%, while the fallback bonus may be set as high as 15% (`requirements.md:215-220`). The fallback removes collateral worth `repayment × (1 + bonus)` and reduces debt by the repayment (`requirements.md:340-341`; `design.md:265-269`). The only cross-parameter bound requires Effective LTV to sit below the liquidation threshold; it does not constrain bonus against threshold (`requirements.md:219-220`).

For $1 of USDC debt repaid against USDC collateral at a 90% threshold and 15% bonus, weighted collateral falls by $1.15 × 0.90 = $1.035 while debt falls by $1. The health deficit grows on every liquidation. Liquidators can strip collateral until the account reaches bad debt and socializes losses through Absorb.

Enforce `liquidation_threshold × (1 + bonus) < 1` for every collateral that can be selected, with a safety margin for rounding and oracle confidence. Specify a close factor, minimum repayment, target post-liquidation health, cross-asset seizure order, and exact share-burning/rounding rules. Reject any governance update that violates the monotonic-health property.

## One unguarded Pyth observation controls borrowing and irreversible liquidation

**High, confirmed.** The oracle rules check feed identity, age, and confidence (`requirements.md:214-221`) and the design computes `price ± confidence` from the current PriceUpdateV2 (`design.md:277-283`). Neither document requires a positive price, bounds the exponent or normalized value, compares spot against Pyth EMA or the prior accepted price, or defines degraded behavior after a large deviation.

A correctly signed but transient outlier can freeze a position, cancel or settle an auction, or permit an unsafe collateral withdrawal before the next update. A nonpositive or extreme mantissa/exponent can also drive subtraction, normalization, or multiplication into aborts; checked arithmetic prevents wraparound but turns the oracle path into a market-wide denial of service.

Define admissible price domains and decimal-normalization bounds, use Pyth EMA or a time-weighted observation for solvency decisions, and add a spot/EMA plus last-good-price deviation circuit breaker. Large deviations should fail closed for borrowing and collateral withdrawal while allowing repay, collateral addition, and a governed emergency liquidation policy. Test signed edge values, exponent extremes, confidence greater than price, and recovery from circuit-breaker activation.

## Exact epoch deltas remain isolatable despite the five-operation gate

**High, confirmed.** The protocol reveals exact cash, supply shares, debt shares, and net flow after a default minimum of five operations (`requirements.md:298-305`). The privacy model itself concedes that “per-asset Epoch deltas can isolate one operation’s amount when an Epoch has few operations” and claims the minimum count mitigates it (`requirements.md:497-506`). The count does not require distinct, unrelated users or minimum economic size.

An observer can bracket a target operation with four known dust operations, trigger the reveal, subtract their own changes, and solve for the target amount from exact aggregate deltas. The same observer can repeat the technique because action identities and timing are public.

Replace the raw operation count with a privacy-budget policy: minimum distinct Positions, minimum elapsed randomized window, anti-Sybil economic cost, and suppression when one participant dominates a cohort. If exact public totals remain required, document that amount confidentiality is not guaranteed against active differencing; otherwise reveal coarse buckets or delayed/time-weighted aggregates instead.

## The global per-asset Pool Lock creates a fee-paid denial-of-service primitive

**High, confirmed.** Every supply, supply withdrawal, borrow, and repay touches one Pool State per asset, and all competing computations receive `AccountBusy` while its Lock is held (`requirements.md:230-236`). The design calls this “less contention” (`design.md:21`) but still serializes every operation for an asset behind an MPC round trip. The only performance acceptance criterion measures 20 runs; there is no throughput, saturation, or starvation target (`requirements.md:176-178`).

A user can continuously submit minimum-value or dust operations and reacquire the Pool Lock as callbacks complete. Legitimate borrowers can miss price windows, repayments and collateral rescues can be delayed, and liquidation settlement can lose the race for the same pool. Even without an attacker, one computation every several seconds is not a demonstrated mainnet capacity model.

Move user intents into independently lockable accounts and batch encrypted deltas into the pool, shard pool state with a formally defined aggregation process, or introduce fair on-chain sequencing that reserves capacity for repay and liquidation. Set measured p95/p99 callback, throughput, backlog, and starvation SLOs under adversarial load, and enforce minimum notionals or fees where they do not leak private amounts.

## Verification-failure cleanup contradicts the documented Arcium callback path

**High, confirmed.** Requirement 2.5 requires Locks to clear when output verification fails (`requirements.md:173-175`), and the design proposes clearing Locks and returning `Ok`, while admitting that a spike still has to determine whether Arcium accepts this (`design.md:146-151`). Arcium’s callback guidance instead handles `verify_output` failure by returning an error, which rolls back Walnut state changes, and Arcium documents separate static failure callbacks for failed computations. Sources: [callback error handling](https://docs.arcium.com/developers/program/callback-type-generation#error-handling) and [post-execution callbacks](https://docs.arcium.com/computations/lifecycle-of-an-arcium-computation#post-execution-callbacks).

If Walnut returns the verification error, its attempted Lock cleanup does not persist. If it returns success after failed verification, it departs from the documented authenticated-output lifecycle and may consume the callback without a verified result. The current fallback is waiting for expiry, which contradicts the acceptance criterion and extends account denial of service.

Define and test a static failure callback that can clear only Locks bound to the failed computation account and offset. Keep success callbacks fail-closed on `verify_output`, and specify idempotent cleanup for callback transaction failure, `OutputTooLarge`, compute exhaustion, queued expiry, and execution failure separately.

## Pending Shield has no unique identity or no-overwrite state machine

**High, confirmed.** Shield first transfers tokens and then records “a Pending Shield” (`requirements.md:186-190`), while Position stores only `pending_shields[2]`, one slot per asset (`design.md:332-338`). The specification says a pending amount may be retried or reclaimed, but it does not require a new shield to fail while that asset’s slot is occupied or bind retry/reclaim to the original computation offset.

After an expired Lock is released, a second shield can overwrite the first pending record before the first amount is reclaimed. Depending on callback ordering, one deposit becomes unclaimable or the wrong amount is credited/refunded. Offset-only Lock checks do not preserve the identity of multiple token deposits.

Use a PendingShield PDA keyed by Position, asset, and monotonic sequence, with immutable amount, source/destination, computation offset, and an explicit `Deposited → Credited | Refunded` state. If one slot per asset is intentional, require and test `NoPendingShield` before accepting another token transfer and bind every retry, callback, and reclaim to that slot’s original offset.

## Multisig governance has no enforced delay or bounded upgrade process

**High, confirmed.** The mainnet plan requires only “a Squads multisig as Admin and upgrade authority” plus parameter review and a pause runbook (`requirements.md:469-483`). The Admin can update asset and protocol parameters, clear pauses, withdraw reserves, and treasury-unshield (`design.md:130-138`); the document claims broad parameter bounds and a Guardian contain mistakes (`design.md:481-488`). There is no timelock, proposal notice, rate-of-change limit, separation of upgrade and treasury roles, or on-chain upgrade freeze policy.

A compromised or coerced signer quorum can install arbitrary program code immediately, change oracle/risk configuration, clear a Guardian pause, and move protocol revenue before users can exit. A checklist does not constrain those actions on chain.

Put routine parameter changes and upgrades behind an enforced timelock with public proposal hashes and a cancellation role. Separate upgrade, risk, treasury, and emergency-pause authorities; let the Guardian pause but not unpause; cap parameter rate-of-change; and define staged upgrade, rollback, and eventual immutability policies. Emergency actions should be narrowly scoped and unable to transfer user value.

## Full-account keeper scans cannot meet liquidation deadlines at scale

**High, confirmed.** The keeper scans “every Position that owes a Debt Asset” at a default 30 checks per minute and rescans all of them after a 2% price move (`requirements.md:373-386`). Discovery uses `getProgramAccounts` and round-robin iteration (`design.md:300-313`). Auctions may last only 60 seconds and fallback only 60 seconds at their lower bounds (`requirements.md:316-341`).

At 100,000 borrowers, one keeper needs more than 55 hours for a normal pass; a price shock requests another full pass. Because checking health costs an MPC computation and the specification defines no caller reward, independent keepers have no guaranteed economic reason to absorb this cost. Bad debt can develop faster than detection and settlement.

Maintain an on-chain debt-position index with resumable cursors, partition work across permissionless keepers, and pay bounded rewards for useful health checks and liquidation progress. Mainnet readiness needs stress-tested detection and settlement SLOs at target account counts, RPC failover, transaction rebroadcast, stale-job alerts, and explicit priority ordering for the riskiest positions.

## Arcium outage recovery is missing from the custody architecture

**High, confirmed.** Mainnet readiness asks only for “Arcium mainnet cluster selection” (`requirements.md:474-482`), while every balance-changing action depends on the single MXE (`requirements.md:26-41`). The keeper releases expired application Locks, but neither specification defines Recovery Peers, MXE key recovery, cluster migration, migration authority, or behavior while migration is in progress.

A prolonged cluster outage or failed key generation leaves users unable to unshield, repay, rescue collateral, or liquidate debt. Lock release restores account availability but cannot execute any encrypted transition, so it is not a funds-recovery mechanism.

Configure and govern Recovery Peers at deployment, separate the MXE migration authority, and add a tested migration runbook with pause semantics, in-flight computation reconciliation, destination-cluster validation, and resume/abort rules. Arcium documents explicit MXE migration and temporary unavailability: [cluster migration](https://docs.arcium.com/developers/deployment#cluster-migration). Mainnet launch should require a devnet migration drill and a quantified recovery-time objective.
